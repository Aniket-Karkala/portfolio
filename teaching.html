Teaching is half the job.

I mentor school students from Grade 4 to Grade 10. Every course below was written for real sessions and rewritten after them.

One day, twelve modules.

ESP32 Fundamentals is a full-day, hands-on workshop for Grade 10 students. It comes with a mentor handbook, an 81-slide student deck, a quiz after each module and a 20-question final. One pin map is used all day, so circuits build up on a single breadboard instead of being torn down between modules.

  1. Meet the microcontrollerWhat a microcontroller is and a tour of the board.
  2. Set up the Arduino IDEInstall, select the board, upload the first sketch.
  3. Digital outputBlink, LED chaser, traffic light and a binary counter.
  4. Digital inputButtons, pull-ups, debouncing and a reaction-time game.
  5. SoundBuzzer tones and programming a melody.
  6. Analog inputPotentiometer, light sensor and hysteresis.
  7. PWM and colourDimming and mixing colours on an RGB LED.
  8. SensorsCapacitive touch, temperature and humidity, ultrasonic distance.
  9. Doing two things at onceTiming with millis() and simple state machines.
  10. InterruptsReacting to events the moment they happen.
  11. Wi-Fi dashboardA web server on the board, in access-point mode.
  12. Capstone buildStudents pick a project from a menu and are graded on a rubric.

Handbooks for the youngest builders.

For a World Robot Olympiad build I wrote two books for Grade 4 and 5 students.

The first is a 20-chapter general volume with a custom diagram for every module, story dialogue, quick-fact tables and mini quizzes. The second is a 25-page deep-dive that explains the real values in the team's own firmware: bus speeds, RFID frequency, servo pulse counts and timing constants.

How I run a project.

  • One sensor at a time. Every part is tested alone on the bench before anything is integrated.
  • Real data early. Students log readings by hand first, so they know what a number means before code hides it.
  • Hours, not dates. Research projects are planned as hour budgets with gates, because school calendars slip.
  • Honest claims. A screening tool is called a screening tool, and limits go in the report.
