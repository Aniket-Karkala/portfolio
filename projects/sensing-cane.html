Sensing cane

A telescoping cane that senses obstacles ahead and potholes below.

The problem.

A plain cane only reports what it touches. Drop-offs, potholes and anything above ground level go unnoticed until it is too late to react.

What is inside.

Inputs

  • YDLIDAR GS5 lidar
  • VL53L5CX 8×8 ToF
  • BNO085 IMU
  • Grip force sensors

Core

  • ESP32
  • Sensor fusion firmware

Outputs

  • Two vibration motors
  • DRV8833 driver
  • 1S 2000 mAh battery

How it works.

  1. A solid-state lidar near the handle measures the distance to whatever is ahead.
  2. An 8×8 time-of-flight imager at the tip looks down at the ground and picks out potholes and steps.
  3. An IMU tracks how the cane is being swung, and two force sensors in the handle read the grip.
  4. An ESP32 combines the readings and warns the user through vibration motors.
  5. The sensor bus crosses the telescoping joint on a four-pin magnetic pogo connector, so the cane still collapses.

What I learned building it.

  • The lidar stays on the fixed handle section, so only the I2C bus has to cross the moving joint.
  • Each sensor was brought up alone on the bench first; the ToF imager ran as a live heatmap before anything was integrated.
  • An MPU-6050 board is kept as a fallback for the IMU.
