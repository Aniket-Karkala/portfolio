Ripening scan box

Three ways of looking at a fruit, compared head to head.

The problem.

Artificially ripened fruit can look ripe outside while the inside lags behind. Which signal screens for it best: colour, near-infrared, or smell?

What is inside.

Inputs

  • RGB camera
  • NoIR camera, 850/940 nm light
  • MQ-3 and MQ-135

Core

  • Four classifiers compared
  • Sensor fusion

Outputs

  • Mismatch score 0–100
  • Three screening classes

How it works.

  1. A portable box images bananas and tomatoes with a normal camera and with a NoIR camera under infrared light.
  2. Two gas sensors sample the volatile compounds the fruit gives off.
  3. Four models are compared: colour only, infrared only, gas only, and all three fused.
  4. The output is a ripening mismatch score from 0 to 100 that sorts fruit into likely natural, suspicious or uncertain.

What I learned building it.

  • It is framed as a screening tool. It makes no claim to detect specific chemicals.
  • A literature review of electronic-nose studies anchors the sensor choices.
