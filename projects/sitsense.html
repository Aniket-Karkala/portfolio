SitSense

A cushion that learns how one person sits before it judges their posture.

The problem.

Posture systems trained on pooled data lose accuracy on people they have never seen. Published results show drops from above 99% on known users to under 78% on new ones.

What is inside.

Inputs

  • 8 force sensors
  • CD74HC4051 multiplexer
  • 2× MPU6050 IMU

Core

  • ESP32-WROOM-32
  • Rule-based detector
  • Per-user calibration

Outputs

  • Posture alerts
  • Logged sessions for analysis

How it works.

  1. Eight force sensors, six in the seat and two in the backrest, are read through a multiplexer.
  2. Two IMUs measure the tilt of the seat and the backrest.
  3. A guided calibration records how each person sits, and thresholds are set relative to that person.
  4. A rule-based detector flags sustained poor sitting, while a pressure-only detector runs alongside it on the same data as a comparison.

What I learned building it.

  • Rules were chosen over machine learning because every threshold has to belong to one person.
  • A reversed sign in the left-right symmetry index made the detector silently never fire. It was found and fixed.
  • The whole study is planned as a 50-hour budget with hour-gated milestones instead of calendar dates.
