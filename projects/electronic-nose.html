Electronic nose for bananas

Smelling the difference between natural and artificial ripening.

The problem.

Bananas ripened with chemical agents are sold as naturally ripened. The gases a banana gives off carry part of the story.

What is inside.

Inputs

  • MQ-3 gas sensor
  • MQ-135 gas sensor
  • ADS1115 16-bit ADC

Core

  • ESP32
  • Calibration and ppm maths

Outputs

  • Serial data log
  • Ripeness comparison

How it works.

  1. An MQ-3 follows ethanol, a by-product of ripening, and an MQ-135 follows ammonia and other volatiles.
  2. Both are read through a 16-bit ADC into an ESP32.
  3. Raw readings are converted to a resistance ratio and then to concentration using curve constants checked against the datasheets.
  4. Bench runs on raw and ripe bananas provide the comparison data.

What I learned building it.

  • Two firmware bugs were found and fixed: the clean-air calibration was off by a factor of 60 for one sensor, and the concentration equation was inverted.
  • The sensors output up to 5 V, so each analog line needs a divider before a 3.3 V input.
  • Humidity shifts these sensors more than anything else, which matters in Mumbai.
  • The build became a two-hour student session with a worksheet built from real bench data.
