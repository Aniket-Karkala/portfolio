Sensors in, something useful out.

I work at Innovation Story in Bandra, where I design embedded and robotics hardware and mentor students from Grade 4 upward through real engineering projects.

Most of what I build starts with a sensor and a question. Can a cane feel a pothole before a foot does? Can two cheap gas sensors tell a naturally ripened banana from a chemically ripened one? Can a camera decide when a cake is done? I prototype on ESP32 and Arduino boards, print the mechanical parts, and test on the bench until the numbers hold up.

I trained as an electronics and telecommunication engineer with a minor in IoT, and spent my internships at Reliance Jio working on network security and cloud. That background shows up in how I debug: measure first, then change one thing.

  • A telescoping cane that notices what a plain cane misses. A solid-state lidar near the handle looks ahead, an 8×8 time-of-flight imager at the tip looks down for potholes, and an IMU and grip sensors watch how the cane is held. Warnings come through vibration. The sensor bus crosses the telescoping joint on a four-pin magnetic pogo connector.

  • Tells artificially ripened bananas from natural ones by the gases they give off. Two gas sensors read through a 16-bit ADC into an ESP32.
